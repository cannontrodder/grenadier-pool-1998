Interaction storyboard · candidate hypothesis

One finger.
One live match.

A finite review sequence for the selected 1998 Sports Broadcast direction. It illustrates how a long-running turn-based match resumes, how the cue may orbit around the cue ball, how power and abort might work under one held finger, and how the pub returns between shots. It does not settle gesture mapping or physics.

Every gesture label below is a prototype hypothesis

Core shot loop

The active table fills the phone. Broadcast graphics sit on the rails and fade while the player touches the table. The shot target never moves under the finger.

Set cue contact

Before aiming, open a touch-sized cue-ball selector away from the eventual shot drag. Move its visible contact point for spin or reset to centre, then close it.

Spin physics is not implied

G02
ABORT

Orbit to aim

While one finger remains down, moving around the cue ball rotates the cue through 360°. The cue-contact setting is collapsed and locked during this gesture.

Shot target stays clear of finger

G03
ABORT

Pull to arm

Candidate mapping: pull farther away from the cue ball for more power. Return the same finger into the marked abort route to disarm; lifting while armed releases.

Distance curve + dead zone undecided

G04
POT · RED / LEFT CENTRE

Release + feedback

Input locks while balls move. Pool sounds lead; a compact result caption appears away from motion and disappears after the state is understood.

No camera move during motion

G05

Pub handover

After balls settle, an optional schematic cutaway shows table left of the back-wall bar, social seating, entrance and toilets, and the next player stepping up. Betty may appear as a stern landlady cameo.

Skippable spatial placeholder

Z01
FINAL · RESULT LOCKED

Postgame knockabout

After the final result, either player can select any remaining ball as the struck ball and smash what is left. Re-rack explicitly starts a fresh game.

Knockabout cannot alter final score

Interruption always disarms

pointercancel, app backgrounding, remote loss of turn, or lost input capture must return to a neutral ready state without taking a shot. This is a behavior requirement, not an animation.
