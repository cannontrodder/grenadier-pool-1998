1998 Sports Broadcast

The table is the event. Tight score captions, bold cuts and a restrained replay grammar make every turn legible while presenting a computer counterpart like a featured player.

First impression“We’re watching a proper match.” It succeeds on clarity and pace; it must keep one human detail in view to avoid becoming anonymous sports television.

Palette + type

Deep navy #0B172A, signal red #E62F3A, cool white and broadcast teal felt. Heavy Arial/Helvetica is compressed, italic and uppercase only for headlines; controls remain plain.

Imagery + texture

Hard-cropped player portrait, flat geometric slashes and clean video color. No faux scanlines over gameplay; low-resolution texture belongs in transitions, never on the balls.

Motion + audio

Score captions wipe on once, replay stingers last under a second, reduced-motion swaps cuts for fades. Cue and pocket audio leads; a short sting is captioned “[match sting].”

Layout + access

Landscape is strongest for G01; portrait stacks score, table and reachable action. A high-contrast top score bar and textual ball/turn state survive color vision differences.

Communicates well

Turn ownership, match status and decisive action. The graphic hierarchy gives spectators and players the same quick read.

Fails when

The system feels like any televised sport; constant stings slow play; red becomes an error color and action color at once; persona is reduced to a headshot statistic.

Recovery states

U01 enters as a lower ticker after G04 settles. M04 changes the score caption to “Reconnecting” with text and timer; it never implies another player’s turn.

Phone question

Can you identify the active player and next action in two seconds, and does the broadcast energy still feel connected to friends at the Grenadier?
